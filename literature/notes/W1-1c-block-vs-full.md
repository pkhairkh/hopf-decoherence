## TL;DR

**The tension is illusory.** Hemelsoet–Voorhaar (HV) compute Hochschild cohomology **with the algebra itself as coefficients**, `HH^s(u_λ, u_λ) = Ext^s_{u_λ ⊗ u_λ^op}(u_λ, u_λ)`, while our conjecture is about Hochschild cohomology **with trivial coefficients**, `HH^2(u_q(g), C) = Ext^2_{u_q(g)^e}(u_q(g), C)` (the C being the trivial module via the counit, used explicitly in `scripts/verify_sl2_hh2.py` lines 14–16, 305–310). These are **distinct cohomology theories** that take wildly different values even in rank one: HV's `HH^{2•}(u_0(sl_2)) ≅ C[N]` is infinite-dimensional (Thm 3.11 of HV quoting [LQ19]), whereas our `dim HH^2(u_q(sl_2), C) = 3` is finite (verified by direct bar complex). Moreover, for trivial coefficients, only the principal block `u_0` contributes to `HH^*(u_q(g), C)` — singular blocks `u_λ, λ ≠ 0` do not contribute at all, because `ε(e_λ) = δ_{λ,0}` for the block idempotents. So the HV singular-block computation is **unrelated** to our conjecture.

## 1. Block decomposition of `u_q(sl_3)` at `ℓ = 3`

### The general decomposition principle (HV, Prop 2.3, lines 122–137)

> **Proposition 2.3 (HV).** There is a decomposition (as two-sided ideals) `u_q(g) ≅ ⊕_{λ∈S} u_λ(g)` where `S` is the set of orbits in `C = {λ ∈ P^+ : 0 ≤ (λ+ρ, θ∨) ≤ ℓ}` under the action of the ℓ-extended affine Weyl group `W_ℓ = W ⋉ ℓP`.

The "blocks" `u_λ` are the two-sided ideals generated by the central idempotents `e_λ`. The block `u_0` (containing the trivial module) is the **principal block**; all others are **singular blocks**.

### The sl_3 alcove at ℓ = 3

For `g = sl_3`, the Coxeter number is `h = 3`. The fundamental weights are `ω_1, ω_2`, with `(ω_i, α_j^∨) = δ_{ij}` and `(ρ, θ^∨) = h - 1 = 2`. The alcove condition `0 ≤ (λ+ρ, θ^∨) ≤ ℓ = 3` gives:

| `λ ∈ P^+` | `(λ+ρ, θ^∨)` | In `C`? |
|---|---|---|
| `0` | 2 | Yes (interior) |
| `ω_1` | 3 | Yes (boundary face) |
| `ω_2` | 3 | Yes (boundary face) |
| `ω_1 + ω_2` | 4 | **No** (outside) |

So the alcove `C` contains exactly three integral points: `C = {0, ω_1, ω_2}`. (Note: `(ℓ-1)ρ = 2ρ = 2ω_1 + 2ω_2` has `(2ρ + ρ, θ^∨) = (3ρ, θ^∨) = 6 > ℓ = 3`, so the Steinberg weight is **outside** the alcove — there is **no Steinberg block** at `ℓ = h = 3`. This is the degenerate `ℓ = h` regime flagged in W1-1a.)

### The orbit structure (open problem)

The number of distinct blocks equals the number of orbits of `W_ℓ = S_3 ⋉ 3P` acting on `C` (under the dot action `w · λ = w(λ + ρ) - ρ`). This orbit computation was flagged as an **open problem** in W1-1a (item W1-1a-β) and is not settled by HV's paper. There are two scenarios:

- **Scenario A (single orbit)**: All three points are `W_ℓ`-linked, giving **one block only** (the principal block). Then `u_q(sl_3) = u_0(sl_3)` and there is no singular block at `ℓ = 3`. The HV singular-block result (Thm 3.2) would not apply at `ℓ = 3`.
- **Scenario B (two orbits)**: The two boundary points `ω_1, ω_2` (linked by the `Z/2` Dynkin symmetry, hence conjugate) form one singular-block orbit, and `0` is a separate principal-block orbit. Then `u_q(sl_3) = u_0 ⊕ u_1` with `u_1 = u_{ω_1} ≅ u_{ω_2}`.

> **Note on the HV setup.** HV state (line 102–103) that they work with `ℓ > h` ("greater than the Coxeter number of `g`"). Their sl_3 results (Thm 3.2, Prop 5.1) are therefore stated for `ℓ ≥ 5` odd, **not** for the degenerate `ℓ = 3 = h` case. Whether Scenario A or B holds at `ℓ = 3` cannot be read off from HV's tables directly.

### Dimensions

HV do not give the dimensions of individual blocks. The full algebra has `dim u_q(sl_3)|_{ℓ=3} = ℓ^{dim g} = 3^8 = 6561`. The principal block `u_0` is typically the largest direct summand; a singular block `u_1` is smaller. **The precise dimensions are not needed for the reconciliation below** — what matters is that, regardless of how many blocks exist, only `u_0` contributes to the trivial-coefficient HH.

## 2. Full vs block cohomology: the precise statement

Let `A = ⊕_{λ∈S} A_λ` be a finite-dimensional algebra decomposed as a direct sum of two-sided ideals (equivalently, as a direct sum of algebras, with each `A_λ` having its own unit `e_λ`, and `e_λ e_μ = δ_{λμ} e_λ`, `Σ e_λ = 1_A`).

### Case (a): Self-coefficient Hochschild cohomology, `HH^*(A, A) = Ext_{A^e}(A, A)`

**Standard decomposition theorem.** As a vector space,

> `HH^*(A, A) ≅ ⊕_{λ∈S} HH^*(A_λ, A_λ).`

This is the version HV use (line 134–135 of HV: "there is a corresponding decomposition of the center: `z(u_q(g)) ≅ ⊕_λ z(u_λ(g))`"; the same direct-sum principle applies to higher HH because the Hochschild complex of `A` decomposes as `C^n(A, A) = Hom(A^{⊗ n}, A) ≅ ⊕_{λ_1, ..., λ_n, μ} Hom(A_{λ_1} ⊗ ... ⊗ A_{λ_n}, A_μ)`, and the differential preserves the diagonal `λ_1 = ... = λ_n = μ` blocks while killing the off-diagonal pieces via the multiplication `A_λ ⋅ A_μ = 0` for `λ ≠ μ` and the unit `e_μ A e_μ = A_μ`).

There are **no cross-terms** in the sense of "mixed-block Hochschild classes". Each block contributes independently.

### Case (b): Trivial-coefficient Hochschild cohomology, `HH^*(A, k) = Ext_{A^e}(A, k)`

Here `k` is the trivial `A`-bimodule, with both left and right action given by an algebra map `ε: A → k` (the counit, for a Hopf algebra).

**Key observation.** Since `e_λ` is a central idempotent, `ε(e_λ) = ε(e_λ^2) = ε(e_λ)^2`, so `ε(e_λ) ∈ {0, 1}`. The constraint `Σ_λ ε(e_λ) = ε(1) = 1` forces **exactly one** block `λ_0` to have `ε(e_{λ_0}) = 1`; all other blocks have `ε(e_λ) = 0`. For the small quantum group with its standard counit (`ε(E_i) = ε(F_i) = 0`, `ε(K_i) = 1`), the block with `ε(e_λ) = 1` is precisely **the principal block `u_0`** (the block containing the trivial module).

It follows that the trivial `A`-bimodule `k` is the same as the trivial `A_0`-bimodule `k` with all other `A_λ` acting by zero. The Hochschild complex `C^*(A, k) = Hom(A^{⊗ *}, k)` then splits as a direct sum, but only the component with **all block indices equal to 0** survives the differential — all other components are killed by the `ε`-terms in the Hochschild differential (because `ε(a) = 0` whenever `a ∈ A_λ` for `λ ≠ 0`).

> **Conclusion (case b).** `HH^*(A, k) ≅ HH^*(A_0, k)`. Only the principal block contributes; singular blocks `A_λ` for `λ ≠ 0` contribute **nothing** to the trivial-coefficient Hochschild cohomology.

### Case (c): Hopf-algebra cohomology `H^*(A, k) = Ext_A(k, k)`

This is yet a third invariant (single-variable Ext, not bimodule Ext). For `A = u_q(g)`, Ginzburg–Kumar (Thm 3.10 of HV, quoting [GK93]) gives `H^{2•}(u_q(g), C) ≅ C[N]` (the coordinate ring of the nilpotent cone), and `H^{2•+1}(u_q(g), C) = 0`. This is **not** a decomposition over blocks of `u_q(g)`; it is a global invariant.

### Summary of the three theories

| Notation | Definition | Block decomposition | Finiteness |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HH^*(A, A)` (self-coefficients) | `Ext_{A^e}^*(A, A)` | `= ⊕_λ HH^*(A_λ, A_λ)` | Typically infinite for Frobenius algebras |
| `HH^*(A, k)` (trivial coefficients) | `Ext_{A^e}^*(A, k)` | `= HH^*(A_0, k)` — only principal block | Can be finite |
| `H^*(A, k)` (Hopf cohomology) | `Ext_A^*(k, k)` | Not a block decomposition; global | `H^{2•} = C[N]` (infinite) |

## 3. Reconciliation: is the HV singular-block result in tension with our conjecture?

**No.** The reconciliation is option **(c)** of the orchestrator's question: the HV object is **a different mathematical object** from our conjecture's object.

### Why option (a) is wrong

Option (a) would say: "the full `HH^2` is the direct sum of block `HH^2`s, the conjecture predicts only the full count, so individual blocks can be much larger — no contradiction." This is **half-true** but misleading. It is true that, **for self-coefficient HH** (`HH^*(u_q(g), u_q(g))`), the full HH is the direct sum of block HHs, so individual blocks can be large. **But our conjecture is not about self-coefficient HH**; it is about trivial-coefficient HH, for which only the principal block contributes at all. The HV singular-block computation does not even enter the sum for our invariant.

### Why option (b) is wrong

Option (b) would say: "the blocks have overlap that needs to be subtracted." There is **no overlap** to subtract. The decomposition `u_q(g) = ⊕_λ u_λ` is a direct sum of two-sided ideals (algebras), with the central idempotents `e_λ` being pairwise orthogonal. The Hochschild complex splits cleanly along the block index; there are no extension problems over `C` (all short exact sequences of vector spaces split). For trivial coefficients, the splitting degenerates further: only the principal block contributes.

### Why option (c) is correct

The HV object is `HH^s(u_λ, u_λ) = Ext^s_{u_λ ⊗ u_λ^op}(u_λ, u_λ)` (Hochschild with self-coefficients, the standard default in the Hochschild-cohomology literature; see also the geometric realisation `HH^s_{C*}(Ñ) ≅ HH^s(u_0)` of Thm 2.5, where the LHS is HH of the variety `Ñ` with diagonal coefficients — this is self-coefficient HH by convention).

Our conjecture's object is `HH^2(u_q(g), C) = Ext^2_{u_q(g)^e}(u_q(g), C)` (Hochschild with trivial coefficients via the counit; explicitly used in `scripts/verify_sl2_hh2.py` lines 14–16, 305–310, and stated in `paper/main.tex` line 69).

These are **different cohomology theories**:
1. **Different coefficient module**: `u_λ` (the algebra) vs `C` (the trivial module via counit).
2. **Different block scope**: for self-coefficient HH, every block contributes; for trivial-coefficient HH, only the principal block `u_0` contributes (`ε(e_λ) = δ_{λ, 0}`).

### Concrete sanity check in rank one

For `g = sl_2`, `ℓ = 3`:
- **HV/LQ**: `HH^{2•}(u_0(sl_2)) ≅ C[N]` as a `C[N]`-module (Thm 3.11 of HV, quoting [LQ19]). In particular, `dim HH^2(u_0(sl_2), u_0(sl_2)) = ∞` (because `C[N]` is infinite-dimensional, and the degree-2 part contains a copy of `C[N]` in bidegree `(2,2)`).
- **Our project**: `dim HH^2(u_q(sl_2), C) = 3` (finite; verified by direct bar complex in `scripts/verify_sl2_hh2.py`).

If the HV object were the same as ours, we would have `3 = ∞` — contradiction. They must be different objects. This confirms the reconciliation at rank one; the rank-two case (sl_3) is no different in principle.

## 4. Coefficient module in Hemelsoet–Voorhaar

### What HV actually compute

HV compute `HH^s(u_λ, u_λ)` — Hochschild cohomology **with the block algebra itself as coefficients** (the standard default when no coefficient module is written). This is the convention throughout the paper:

- **Theorem 2.5** (line 153, quoting [BL07]): `HH^s_{C*}(Ñ) ≅ HH^s(u_0)`. The LHS is the `C*`-equivariant Hochschild cohomology of the Springer resolution, which by standard convention is `Ext^s_{Ñ × Ñ}(O_Δ, O_Δ)` — self-coefficients. The RHS `HH^s(u_0)` inherits this convention.
- **Theorem 3.2** (line 707): "Let `u^p_λ(sl_3)` be the block... For `s ≥ 2`, `HH^s(u^p_λ(sl_3))` is given by the following tables" — no coefficient module written, hence default = self-coefficients.
- **Theorem 3.10** (line 1115, Ginzburg–Kumar): `H^{2•}(u_q(g), C) ≅ C[N]`. Here `H^•` (not `HH^•`!) is explicitly written with `C` coefficients, because this is Hopf-algebra cohomology `Ext_{u_q(g)}(C, C)` (single-variable Ext over `u_q(g)`), **not** Hochschild cohomology. HV explicitly distinguish `H^•(u_q(g), C)` (Hopf, trivial coeff) from `HH^•(u_λ)` (Hochschild, self-coeff).
- **Line 1120**: "Since `H^•(u_q(g), C)` acts on `HH^•(u_q(g))`, for each block the `G`-representation `HH^•(u^p_λ(g))` is actually a `G × C*`-equivariant coherent sheaf on the nilpotent cone." This is the smoking gun: the `H^•(u_q(g), C) = C[N]` (Ginzburg–Kumar) acts on `HH^•(u_q(g))` as a coefficient ring. The action of `H^•(A, k) = Ext_A(k, k)` on `HH^•(A, A) = Ext_{A^e}(A, A)` is the standard "Centres / cohomology acts on Hochschild" structure (Gerstenhaber; see also Schweigert–Woike). If `HH^•` here were trivial-coefficient, the action would be of a different kind.

### Three cohomology theories — explicit distinction

HV (and the surrounding literature on small quantum groups) work with **three distinct** cohomology theories that are easy to conflate:

| HV's notation | Our project's notation | Definition | HV result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `H^•(u_q(g), C)` | `H^•(u_q(g), C)` (Hopf cohomology) | `Ext_{u_q(g)}^*(C, C)` | `H^{2•} = C[N]`, `H^{2•+1} = 0` (Thm 3.10, [GK93]) |
| `HH^•(u_λ)` (HV default) | `HH^•(u_λ, u_λ)` (Hochschild self-coeff) | `Ext_{u_λ^e}^*(u_λ, u_λ)` | Thm 3.2, Prop 5.1 — usually infinite-dim as `C[N]`-module |
| — (not computed by HV) | `HH^•(u_q(g), C)` (Hochschild trivial-coeff) | `Ext_{u_q(g)^e}^*(u_q(g), C)` | **Our conjecture**: `dim HH^2 = C(n+1,2) + 2|Φ^+|` |

The trivial-coefficient Hochschild cohomology (third row) is **not computed anywhere in HV's paper**. It is also not computed in [LQ16], [LQ17], [LQ19], [BL07], or [GK93] — these all work with either self-coefficient HH or Hopf cohomology.

### Sanity check from `H^•` vs `HH^•` notation

The use of `H^•` (one variable) vs `HH^•` (two variables, bimodule) is a notational tell:
- `H^*(H, M) = Ext_H^*(k, M)` — Hopf-algebra cohomology. One module variable.
- `HH^*(H, M) = Ext_{H^e}^*(H, M)` — Hochschild cohomology. Bimodule variable.
- `HH^*(H) := HH^*(H, H)` — default Hochschild (self-coefficients).

HV consistently write `HH^s(u_λ)` (no coefficient written) for self-coefficient Hochschild, and `H^•(u_q(g), C)` (with `C`) for Hopf cohomology with trivial coefficients. **They never write `HH^s(u_λ, C)` for Hochschild with trivial coefficients** — that is our project's invariant, and it does not appear in their paper.

## 5. Bottom line

**The Hemelsoet–Voorhaar singular-block computation is unrelated to our conjecture.** It computes a different cohomology theory (Hochschild with self-coefficients `HH^*(u_1, u_1)`, the standard default in the literature) of a different sub-object (the singular block `u_1`), while our conjecture concerns Hochschild with trivial coefficients `HH^2(u_q(g), C)` of the full algebra (and only the principal block `u_0` contributes to this invariant, since the trivial module factors through the counit which annihilates the singular-block idempotents).

**One-paragraph explanation for a physicist.** Think of `u_q(g)` as a direct sum of matrix-algebra-like pieces (the blocks `u_λ`), and think of Hochschild cohomology as a kind of "operator algebra cohomology" that measures the infinitesimal deformations of the algebra. There are two natural choices of "coefficient module" for this cohomology: the algebra itself (which sees every block, and is typically infinite-dimensional because each block has internal structure), or the trivial one-dimensional module given by the counit (which sees only the block containing the vacuum, and can be finite-dimensional). The Hemelsoet–Voorhaar paper computes the former — Hochschild cohomology of an individual block with itself as coefficients — and their answer for the singular block `u_1(sl_3)` is large (`≥ 59` in degree 2). Our conjecture is about the latter — Hochschild cohomology of the full algebra with trivial coefficients — and predicts `9` in degree 2 for `u_q(sl_3)`. These are not in conflict: they are different cohomology theories, and even within a fixed theory, the singular block does not contribute to the trivial-coefficient invariant. A loose analogy: computing the Euler characteristic of a connected component of a space (HV) versus computing the Euler characteristic of the whole space with coefficients in a sheaf supported on one component (ours) — both can be valid, and one does not constrain the other.
