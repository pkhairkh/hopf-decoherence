Abstract
The abelian and monoidal structure of the category of smooth weight modules over a non-integrable
affine vertex algebra of rank greater than one is an interesting, difficult and essentially wide open problem.
Even conjectures are lacking. This work details and tests such a conjecture for L− 3 (sl3 ) via a logarithmic
2
Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondence.
H
We first investigate the representation theory of U i (sl3 ), the unrolled restricted quantum group of
sl3 at fourth root of unity. In particular, we analyse its finite-dimensional weight category, determining
Loewy diagrams for all projective indecomposables and decomposing all tensor products of irreducibles.
Our motivation is that this category is conjecturally braided tensor equivalent to a category of WA02 (2)modules. Here, WA02 (2) is an orbifold of the octuplet vertex algebra WA2 (2) of Semikhatov, the latter
being the natural sl3 -analogue of the well known triplet algebra. Moreover, WA02 (2) is the parafermionic
coset of the affine vertex algebra L− 3 (sl3 ).
2

We formulate an explicit conjecture relating the representation theory of WA02 (2) and U H
i (sl3 ) and work
out the resulting structures of the corresponding L− 3 (sl3 )-modules. In particular, we obtain conjectural
2
Loewy diagrams for the latter’s projective indecomposables and decompositions for the fusion products
of its irreducibles. These products coincide with those recently computed via Verlinde’s formula. Finally,
we give analogous results for WA2 (2).

Introduction

Two excellent sources of braided tensor categories are the representation categories of modules of quantum
groups and of vertex operator algebras. Here, we are mostly interested in categories that are not semisimple.
Besides being interesting in their own right, such categories give rise to rich invariants of 3-manifolds [CGP14]
and they naturally emerge from quantum field theories [CDGG]. In this work, we study the case of the
unrolled restricted quantum group of sl3 at fourth root of unity, U H
i (sl3 ), and its connection to the simple
affine vertex operator algebra of sl3 at level − 23 , L− 32 (sl3 ). Let us start by reviewing the state of the art.

1.1

Affine vertex algebras at admissible level

Let g be a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra. Then, to any k ∈ C, one associates the simple

affine vertex algebra of g at level k, which we denote by Lk (g). If k is a non-negative integer, then Lk (g) is
rational and lisse, hence its module category is a semisimple modular tensor category [Hu08b]. It is a major
research goal to thoroughly understand the module categories of Lk (g) for general k. A relatively accessible,
though still extremely challenging, case is when k is an admissible level [KW88].
This goal entails first classifying the irreducible modules, second understanding extensions between them
and third establishing a vertex tensor category structure and determining its properties. There are of course
different types of categories of modules that one can study. The simplest one is the category of ordinary
modules, these being the positive-energy modules whose conformal weight spaces are finite-dimensional. For
k admissible, this category is a semisimple vertex tensor category [Ar16, CHY18] for which rigidity is also
mostly established [Cr19, CKL22, CGL].
It turns out that when k is not a non-negative integer, Lk (g) usually has many modules that are not positiveenergy or have infinite-dimensional conformal weight spaces. Among other reasons, one must then extend
the category of ordinary modules to include some of these in order to obtain a modular-invariant partition
function [KW88, CR12, CR13b]. There is therefore significant current effort to understand these extended
categories of Lk (g)-modules, particularly those that include the so-called relaxed highest-weight modules
[FST98, RW15], see for example [Ad16, RW15a, AFR17, RSW18, Ad19, KR19, CKLR18, AP19, KR21,
AW20, FMR20, FK21, AKR21, FKR21, FMK21, KRW21].
In the case g = sl2 , the irreducible positive-energy modules were classified in [AM95]. Reducible positiveenergy modules were subsequently considered in many works, including [Ga01, Ri11, CR12, CR13b, KR19].
Moreover, modules that are not positive-energy are often obtained by applying spectral flow functors to
those that are. In fact, all irreducible weight modules with finite-dimensional weight spaces can be obtained
in this fashion [FT01]. Non-trivial extensions between such spectrally flowed modules were introduced in
[Ga01, Ri11] and a general construction of Adamović [Ad19] covers all the indecomposable projective modules
[ACK] (in the category of weight modules).
Beyond g = sl2 , the structures of indecomposable projective modules are currently unknown. There are
however many conjectural correspondences between categories of modules of certain quantum groups and
vertex operator algebras. These are often called logarithmic Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondences and we will

reasonably accessible, this is a promising direction to deduce otherwise unimaginable structural conjectures
for indecomposable projective L− 23 (sl3 )-modules.
Eventually, one would not only like to understand the linear categories of an affine vertex algebra, but also
their monoidal structures. For example, one would like to obtain fusion rules for the irreducible modules.
Verlinde’s formula [Ve88] is a particularly nice way to obtain (Grothendieck) fusion rules, though it is only
established for rational and lisse vertex operator algebras [Hu08a]. There is nevertheless a generalised Verlinde
conjecture [CR13a, RW15b] that has been successfully applied to Lk (sl2 ) at admissible levels [CR12, CR13b].
This conjectural formula has been recently lifted to L− 32 (sl3 ) in a companion paper [KRW21], so that the
tensor product decompositions of U H

may be compared.

1.2

Logarithmic Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondences

The category of ordinary modules of the affine vertex operator algebra Lk (g), with g simple and k+h∨ ∈
/ Q≥0 ,
is equivalent as a braided tensor category to the category of finite-dimensional modules of Lusztig’s quantum

g. This is the original Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondence [KL93a, KL93b, KL94a, KL94b] established in the
1990s.

Related progress on the mathematics of logarithmic conformal field theory was made in the mid-2000s
[FGST06a, FGST06b, FGST06c, FGST07]. These papers conjectured a logarithmic Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondence between the representation categories of the triplet algebras WA1 (p) (parameterised by an integer
p ≥ 2) and the restricted quantum group U q (sl2 ) at q = eπi/p . It was later noticed that the correspon-

dence is in fact between the triplet algebras and a quasi-Hopf algebra whose underlying algebra is the
quantum group. One calls this a quasi-Hopf modification of the quantum group [GR17, CGR20]. In this

instance, the quantum group category was quickly understood and it thus provided reliable conjectures about
WA1 (p)-module categories, in particular their abelian structures and fusion rules. It then took many works
[AM08, AM07, KS11, NT11, TW15, CGR20, MY20, CLR21, GN21] to completely settle these conjectures.

This unrolled/singlet correspondence is of significant interest. One reason is that very interesting vertex
algebras, called Bp -algebras [CRW14], may be realised as simple current extensions of WA01 (p) and a rank-one
Heisenberg vertex algebra. For p = 2, one gets the βγ-vertex algebra [Ri10]; for p = 3, one gets L−4/3 (sl2 )

Bp -algebras are important in physics as they arise as chiral algebras of certain Argyres–Douglas theories

[BR18, Cr18, ACGY21], these being a type of four-dimensional superconformal quantum field theory.
The quantum group correspondence was then used to give a complete, but of course still conjectural, description of the category of smooth weight modules of all the Bp -algebras [ACKR20]. In particular, fusion

rules were conjectured via Verlinde’s formula and, even better, the normalised entries of the modular S-

transformation corresponding to the generalised Verlinde formula were found to coincide with the normalised
modified traces of open Hopf links, as was already anticipated in [CMR18, CG17, GR19]. In summary, these
logarithmic Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondences between quantum group categories and categories of modules
of vertex algebras, both associated to sl2 , have been invaluable for better understanding the representation
theory of the vertex algebras. It is thus reasonable to extend this program to higher ranks.
In this direction, there is a natural generalisation of the triplet algebras called the Feigin–Tipunin algebras
[FT10]. They are parameterised by a simply laced simple Lie algebra g and an integer p ≥ 2. The corresponding quantum groups are expected to be quasi-Hopf modifications of the restricted quantum groups U q (g) at

q = eπi/p . The unrolled version should moreover correspond to the orbifold of the Feigin–Tipunin algebra by
the maximal torus of the Lie group of g. Unfortunately, very few results have thus far been established for
these vertex algebras, see [Se14, Se13, CM17, Su21, AMW20, Su21b].

As in the case of g = sl2 , there exist extensions of the tensor product of these Feigin–Tipunin orbifolds by
Heisenberg vertex algebras of the same rank as g [Cr17]. These extensions also arise in physics as chiral
algebras of Argyres–Douglas theories [BT17, Cr17]. The very first higher-rank example is g = sl3 and p = 2.
In this case, the extension was identified by Adamović as L− 32 (sl3 ) [Ad16]. It is also worth pointing out that
a different extension, obtained by first tensoring with a rank-one Heisenberg vertex algebra, gives the small
N = 4 superconformal algebra at central charge −9.
The quantum group associated to the orbifold WA02 (2) of the Feigin–Tipunin algebra for sl3 and p = 2 is
UH
i (sl3 ). General results for these unrolled restricted quantum groups and their uprollings were obtained
in [GP13, CGP14, CGP15, Ru19, CR20], but the explicit structures of the projective indecomposables and
decompositions of tensor products were not known. We thus decided to study this example in order to
compare our findings with the recent results of [KRW21] on the representation theory of L− 32 (sl3 ), via the
conjectural logarithmic Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondence.

in Section 4.4 with the conjectured Loewy diagrams for the indecomposable projective L− 23 (sl3 )-modules
reported in Figure 7. This gives the first predictions for the structures of these projective covers and will be
invaluable guides for their subsequent construction and analysis.
We also test the tensor part of the conjectured equivalence by using the tensor product decompositions for
UH
i (sl3 ), given in Section 3.3, to predict fusion rules for L− 23 (sl3 ) in Section 4.5 and checking that they
descend to the Grothendieck fusion rules recently calculated in [KRW21]. This is a highly non-trivial check
of both our Conjecture 4.3 and the standard Verlinde conjecture for logarithmic models [CR13a, RW15b].
To conclude, we again use vertex algebra extension theory to study the representation theory of WA2 (2). This

to be C2 -cofinite. We verify in Section 4.6, again assuming Conjecture 4.3, that there are finitely many
irreducibles (up to isomorphism) and that all are ordinary. Predictions for their fusion rules are deduced and
Loewy diagrams for the projective indecomposables are proposed in Figure 8.

Every module in C admits a composition series. While this series is not unique, they all have the same length.
Moreover, the set of isomorphism classes of the irreducible quotients (the composition factors) is uniquely
fixed by the isomorphism class of the module. The set of isomorphism classes of the quotients (the Verma
factors) of a standard filtration is likewise unique, though not every module in C admits such a series.
Given a module V ∈ C which admits a standard filtration, denote by (V : Mλ ) the multiplicity with which
Mλ appears in the set of Verma factors of any standard filtration of V . Similarly, denote by [V : Lλ ] the
multiplicity with which Lλ appears in the set of irreducible quotients of any composition series of V .
Proposition 2.11 ([Ru19, Proposition 4.12]). BGG reciprocity holds for the category C . In other words,
(Pλ : Mµ ) = [Mµ : Lλ ] for all λ, µ ∈ h∗ .

A useful consequence of Theorem 3.2 is the classification of indecomposable extensions of irreducibles in
C . Self-extensions are ruled out as they are necessarily non-weight, hence not in C . Such an extension is
therefore a highest-weight module, hence a Verma quotient, or the dual of a highest-weight module. Either
way, the extension is unique up to isomorphism.

and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:
• λ is atypical of degree 1 and µ = λ ± αi , where i = 1, 2, 3 satisfies λi ∈ 2Z + 1.
• λ is atypical of degree 2 with λ1 , λ2 ∈ 2Z + 1 and µ = λ ± α1 , λ ± α2 , λ − α1 + 2α3 , λ − α2 + 2α3 .
• λ is atypical of degree 2 with λ3 ∈ 2Z + 1 and µ = λ ± αi , λ + αi − 2α3 , where i = 1, 2 satisfies λi ∈ 2Z + 1.
The blocks of U H
i (sl3 ) are thus as follows. If λ is typical, then Lλ generates a semisimple block in which it is

the only irreducible. There are three one-parameter families of blocks corresponding to λ atypical of degree
1, one for each positive root αi . The parameter may be taken as either of the typical λi ∈ C \ Z. Finally,

there is a unique block for λ atypical of degree 2. This is the principal block as it includes L0 .

In this section, a Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondence is conjectured between the category of finite-dimensional
weight U H
i (sl3 )-modules and a module category of a vertex operator algebra. More precisely, the conjecture is

that this correspondence takes the form of an equivalence of braided tensor categories. The vertex operator
algebra appearing in this correspondence is not L− 32 (sl3 ), but its parafermionic coset. This is of course
the commutant of the rank-2 Heisenberg subalgebra of L− 32 (sl3 ) generated by the fields associated with the
Cartan subalgebra of sl3 .
The reason why one might expect a Kazhdan–Lusztig correspondence involving this parafermionic vertex
operator algebra is that it has recently been shown [Ad16, AMW20] to be isomorphic to WA02 (2), a rank-2
analogue of the singlet algebras WA01 (p), p ∈ Z≥2 [Ka91]. This isomorphism extends the known coincidences

between the Lk (sl2 )-parafermions, with k = − 21 and − 34 , and the WA01 (p), with p = 2, 3 respectively [Ad05,
Ri11] (as well as between k = − 32 and the bosonic orbifold of an N = 1 “supersinglet” algebra [Ad16, ACR18]).

WA02 (2) is one of a family, parametrised by p ∈ Z≥2 and an ADE Dynkin diagram, of vertex algebras

introduced by Feigin and Tipunin [FT10], see also [CM17]. Aside from the A1 case (the singlet algebras), the

representation theories of these vertex operators algebras have not yet received much attention. However,
that of the singlet algebras is very well understood, see [AM07, AM09, TW15, RW14, CMY20b]. In this case,
a conjectural braided tensor equivalence has been proposed [CGP14, CM14] and studied [CMR18, CGR20,
GN21, CLR21]. It connects a category of modules over WA01 (p) with the category of finite-dimensional weight
πi/p

to a braided tensor equivalence between the category of finite-dimensional weight U H

appropriate category of WA02 (2)-modules. Here, this conjecture is made explicit by comparing with the
